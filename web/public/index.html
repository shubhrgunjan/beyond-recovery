WHAT THE EXPERIMENTS ACTUALLY SAY

A better baseline changed the story.

These are exploratory simulation results, not a demonstrated recovery-preservation algorithm.

01 / TASK CONTROL

Many “recovery failures” were ordinary control problems.

After improving yaw response, conventional map planning raised completion in the 0.75 m entrance test from 11/48 to 44/48. Each cell reused eight parent seeds.

Inspect the calibration study ↗
02 / PHYSICAL SUPPORT

More empty space is not always better.

Under stronger impulses, open-space trials lost balance in 79/96 cases; the 0.90 m barrier condition had 0/96. Body contact supplied support, with substantial contact-force costs.

Inspect contact evidence ↗
03 / LIVE RECOVERY

Standing up and returning to work are separate events.

A live trip test recorded balance loss at 8.98 s, sustained standing at 11.62 s, and original-task advancement at 21.18 s. No recovery-time pose reset.

A CONVENTIONAL BASELINE MATTERS

Test the explanation before building the algorithm.

The first map planner barely helped with weak yaw tracking. Once turning worked, the same planner resolved many apparent failures. Original and corrected protocols remain available, including the negative results.

What remains unproven

No learned preservation policy, calibrated safety guarantee, selectable recovery portfolio or real-robot validation is claimed. Seeded branches share parent states; 1,808 trials are not 1,808 independent environments. Simulator contact forces are not injury estimates.
